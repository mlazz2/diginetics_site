Customers
Who Elijah Underwriting Intelligence is for.
Firms that underwrite real property decisions every week: sign the listing, fund the loan, vote at IC, or price the policy. Elijah gives each of them a defensible underwriting layer built from their market data and parsed property records.
Brokerages · Primary ICP

Independent regional residential brokerages running listing intelligence before and after sign.

71% of US agents have zero active listings. Marketing budgets get split evenly across databases that should not be treated equally. Sellers want a price defense. Brokers want a Take or Pass call before they commit brokerage resources. Comp sheets alone do not get any of that done.

Pain points
  • Pricing decisions made on instinct, defended after days on market pile up.
  • Marketing spend wasted locally when the real buyer is three feeder markets away.
  • No portfolio view of which listings need a pricing conversation this week.
  • Expired listings that cost reputation and broker time.
What Elijah delivers
  • Listing Underwrite Card per appointment: Take, Pass, pricing band, DOM forecast.
  • Buyer-origin feeder ZIPs ranked by tier with channel and seasonality guidance.
  • Active Listing Monitor with green, yellow, red posture per listing.
  • Price-cut playbook and re-marketing triggers through close.
  • Broker portfolio queue: which listings need action now.

Value to the brokerage

One avoided expired listing covers the annual cost several times over.

Before sign: walk into the appointment with defensible pricing bands and a Take or Pass call. After sign: stop guessing on cuts and carry. Know when DOM is abnormal and where to re-target buyers.

CRE and Capital
Developers, lenders, and acquirers on the same underwriting chassis.

CRE is a loop: developer sponsors, construction lenders, GP acquirers, senior lenders, disposition brokers. Elijah attaches defensible market intelligence at each decision point.

CRE Development

Developers and sponsors voting at IC on assumptions they cannot defend.

Every deal memo carries rent growth, vacancy, cap rate, and exit assumptions. Most cannot survive a cross-check against validated market bands. IC votes on models, not markets.

Pain points
  • Assumption sets copied from the last deal, not the current market.
  • No standardized PASS, WARN, or PARTIAL gate before the vote.
  • Argus outputs that nobody can audit against live market data.
  • Thin comp sets on secondary-market and non-gateway metro deals.
What Elijah delivers
  • CRE Deal Underwrite Card with assumption audit on every active deal.
  • Market band cross-check vs sponsor model with IC-ready exhibit export.
  • Document parsing on rent rolls, T-12s, and appraisal excerpts.
  • Weekly metro regime brief: supply, rent, rate relationships in plain language.
Lending

CRE lenders and debt funds reviewing collateral without market context.

Credit teams receive borrower models and appraisals. The question is whether assumptions hold against what the market actually does. Most reviews lack a defensible cross-check attached to the loan file.

Pain points
  • Borrower models accepted without validated market cross-check.
  • Appraisal assumptions that drift from current regime.
  • No audit trail when a loan goes south on market assumptions.
  • Manual comp scrubbing on every collateral review.
What Elijah delivers
  • Collateral review card: borrower assumptions vs validated market bands.
  • Parsed rent rolls and operating statements attached to the review.
  • 2 to 4 page exhibit for the credit file with PASS, WARN, or PARTIAL posture.
  • Regime alerts when market shifts affect outstanding collateral.
Acquisitions

Buy-and-sell operators running pipelines without a Take or Pass gate.

Acquisition shops evaluate dozens of deals. Most pass or pursue based on gut and spreadsheet comps. Seller motivation, estate context, and clean comp pools rarely surface before the offer.

Pain points
  • Offers made without probate, estate, or ownership context.
  • Comp pools polluted by non-arms-length and inherited sales.
  • No standardized verdict across the acquisition team.
  • Disposition timing guessed without DOM and absorption data.
What Elijah delivers
  • Take, Pass, or Take at price on every property in the pipeline.
  • County deed parsing for ownership timeline and distress signals.
  • Probate and estate flags for seller motivation context.
  • Disposition guidance: pricing band and DOM forecast for exit planning.
Insurance

Carriers and MGAs pricing property exposure without localized context.

Commercial P&C underwriting depends on property condition, ownership stability, subdivision risk mix, and localized regime shifts. Most models use territory averages, not property-level enrichment.

Pain points
  • Territory-level pricing without property-specific enrichment.
  • Ownership and distress signals buried in county records.
  • Regime shifts (rates, inventory) not translated to portfolio exposure.
  • Black-box actuarial scores that underwriters cannot defend.
What Elijah delivers
  • Property enrichment: ownership timeline, estate flags, subdivision mix.
  • Localized regime briefs tied to zip and peril exposure.
  • Document parsing on property records and loss history uploads.
  • Audit-readable underwriting cards attached to the policy file.
